# Why I Chose a Static Website for My Business — And Why I'm Not Going Back to WordPress

If you ask most web developers about static websites, you'll probably hear the same answer:
*"They're great for portfolios or landing pages, but not for real businesses."*
For years, static websites have been seen as simple HTML pages—fast, yes, but too limited for blogs, online stores, or growing businesses.
I used to think the same.
Throughout my career, I've worked with different platforms, including WordPress, Joomla, and PrestaShop. Each one has its own strengths, weaknesses, and learning curve.
WordPress is incredibly flexible, but that flexibility comes with responsibility. Plugin updates, database backups, security patches, performance optimization, and compatibility issues all become part of maintaining the website.
Joomla offers powerful features but can feel overwhelming for many business owners.
PrestaShop is excellent for e-commerce, but it introduces another layer of complexity with its own architecture, hosting requirements, and maintenance.
After working with all of them, I realized something important.
**A good developer shouldn't fall in love with a specific technology. A good developer chooses the solution that best serves the business.**
So when it came time to build my own website, I asked myself a different question:
**What solution will be the fastest, the most secure, the easiest to maintain, and the most cost-effective over the next several years?**
The answer surprised even me.
A static website.
Not because it was easier.
And certainly not because I couldn't build something else.
I chose it because, after working with multiple CMS platforms, I understood exactly what I needed—and what I didn't.
Today my website loads almost instantly. It doesn't rely on a traditional database, has a much smaller attack surface, requires very little maintenance, and costs significantly less to operate than many conventional websites.
But the most interesting part is this:
It's no longer "just" a static website.
It includes an AI assistant.
It supports multiple languages.
It integrates with cloud services.
It can connect to contact forms, payment providers, analytics, APIs, and virtually any modern online service.
The pages may be static.
The business behind them certainly isn't.

## The Biggest Myth About Static Websites

Whenever I mention that my website is static, I almost always get the same question:
**"But how do you update your content without an admin panel?"**
A few years ago, that was a fair concern.
Today, I don't think it is.
People still imagine editing a static website by manually opening files, writing Markdown, committing changes with Git, and deploying everything themselves.
That's no longer how my workflow looks.
Instead, I'm building an automated publishing system around my website.
With a single click, I can publish a new article. The system formats the content, generates SEO metadata, creates translations when needed, prepares the required files, builds the website, and deploys everything automatically.
The same idea extends beyond the blog.
One piece of content can become a website article, an Instagram post, a TikTok script, or content for other platforms without repeating the same work over and over again.
That's why I no longer see the lack of a traditional admin panel as a disadvantage.
For a well-organized business, an efficient workflow is far more valuable than another dashboard filled with buttons.

## Why Maintenance Costs Are Lower

People are often surprised when they ask how much it costs to maintain my website.
As the developer of my own project, my recurring expenses are minimal.
I pay for my domain name.
That's essentially my website's address on the internet.
I also use HTTPS, which provides an encrypted and secure connection between visitors and my website. Besides protecting data, HTTPS builds trust with users and has become a standard expectation for modern websites.
Beyond that, there are very few ongoing technical costs.
Content still needs to be created, whether your website runs on WordPress, a static site generator, or any other platform.
Someone still has to write articles.
Someone still has to update products.
Someone still has to publish new information.
The real difference isn't whether a person is involved.
It's how much repetitive work that person has to do.
Automation dramatically reduces that workload.

## Static Doesn't Mean Limited

One criticism is absolutely fair.
Interactive features like user accounts, booking systems, payment processing, or advanced forms usually require third-party services or custom APIs.
For me, that's actually an advantage.
Instead of relying on one monolithic system to do everything, I can choose the best service for each specific task.
That approach keeps my website lightweight while allowing it to grow whenever my business needs new functionality.

## Static Doesn't Mean Cheap to Build

Another common misconception is that static websites are easy to create.
They're not.
The visitor only sees a fast-loading page.
They don't see the planning behind it.
Information architecture.
SEO strategy.
Image optimization.
Multilingual content.
Automation.
Accessibility.
Deployment pipelines.
Performance optimization.
All of that happens long before the first visitor opens the website.
That's why clients aren't paying for HTML files.
They're investing in architecture, experience, and a system designed to perform reliably for years.

## So Why Did I Choose a Static Website?

Because I wasn't looking for the trendiest technology.
I was looking for the smartest business decision.
I wanted a website that loads instantly.
Ranks well in search engines.
Costs very little to maintain.
Can be automated.
Scales easily.
And lets me focus on growing my business instead of maintaining software.
Does that mean WordPress is a bad choice?
Absolutely not.
It's still an excellent solution for many projects.
But for my business—and for many small businesses—a modern static website offers something incredibly valuable:
More speed.
Less maintenance.
Better security.
Lower operating costs.
And with today's automation and AI tools, many of the traditional limitations of static websites are no longer limitations at all.
That's exactly what I hope this website demonstrates.
